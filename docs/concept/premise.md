Main idea is that it's a game based on a minimal list of friends (5-10 people) where all the characters are variations of the playsers themselves.

There is a full 4 day cycle that happens simultaneously for all players:

1. Prompt: Each player gets a prompt for a challenger each day that is randomly drawn from the other players (never themselves). The player needs to put in a title among with randomly drawn name; either suffixed or prefixed. There should be a relatively tight character window (e.g. 35 characters). In the future there may be a theme to match (e.g. represented by the background) but for now it's just an open prompt each day. If missed by the player, a prompt will be randomly selected from a list of ~500 pregenerated prompts from the database.
2. Sketch: Once each day, the player should draw a "challenger" (enemy) based on the prompt (see previous step) that a player party will face (see next step). This is the main way of the game to generate new content so the focus of features, functionality etc. should be om that part. The player is given one of the prompts of stage 1 (but again, never their own name), handed round at random afresh every day: never their own prompt, and never a prompt of themselves unless there's no way round it (everyone wrote one of the same player). Who boosts which challengers and who fights which one are shuffled afresh every day too, keeping the fairness of steps 3 and 4. Servers that were running when this came in switched the day after it went live (speed sessions running then kept the roster order to their end); before, everyone drew the previous player's prompt and needs to draw that prompt in a given time (90 seconds). The timer only starts once they press **Ready** on top of the canvas: until then they can read the prompt (and the question and theme behind its info button) and still leave, but not draw. Once the timer runs there's no backing out to start over: the app has no back button until the drawing is done, and on the web the browser asks before the page is closed or reloaded (if time runs out with nothing drawn, leaving is allowed again). Once the player presses Done, the clock stops and the drawing is saved first (the screen says so; if saving fails, Done tries again). Only then does the player see the drawing with its title in the blue nameplate and the names of the players who sent a hurry against it (with their short messages), all on one screen to share as a screenshot. This is a "mandatory" step and the only thing a player should do each day. Without this step no new content can be generated. So this step should be front and center and be somewhat mandatory before doing anything else in the game. It should also be the step that's rewarded the most.
3. Boost: On day 3, a player can go through all the challengers one by one (except for the one they will face and the one they drew) and decide how much to boost them. The challenger starts with 3 stars, and every player has 3 boost stars a day to spread across their challengers to boost, all of them on one at most. From the second week on, how the week before's fights went balances that number, between 3 and 6 a day, from the Sunday before each week, as Sunday's challengers are fought in the new week (see [Boost Balance](./boost-balance.md)); the boost screen shows the stars to give, and on Sunday that they're next week's. Only match-ups where the player drew neither the enemy/challenger nor selected the fighting party members should be shown to them for voting. It should a random order but "fair" (one person gets one challenger first, another player a different challenger etc.). The player can freely navigate between the three challengers to decide which to boost. ~~They can't go back to previous challengers after boosting (or not boosting) them. If they haven't used all their boost by the end they have to use it all on the last one (they should see how many there are).~~ There should be a drag and drop control at the bottom of the screen for boosting. The stars moved are only saved with the **Done** button (one save for all of them); leaving the screen with stars not saved asks first whether to save them, discard them or keep boosting (on the web, the browser asks too before the page is closed). If the player doesn't boost (or only partially) it should be spread at random. A player who didn't draw their challenger the day before can't boost at all that day (see [Skipped Days](./skipped-days.md)). To decrease complexity, all players are only given 3 random (but fair) challengers to choose from (top 3 in their random list, the rest are omitted)
4. Challenge: Once a day, players can face challengers/enemies from the day before (see previous step) by assembling a party of legends from their roster. The roster of the player will increase over time but the main idea is that each player can select up to 4 legends as "matchups" against challengers. These challengers will apear larger at the upper part of the screen, similar to a boss in a traditional Final Fantasy game, while the legends will slot in as party members on the bottom with both the enemy as well as the party member titles cleary visible.  Each player picks from their pool of heroes. The goal is to reach more stars than the challenger (which you don't know yet). E.g. if you pick 4 one-star characters, you beat the challenger which has three stars. For equal stars, the challenger wins. Duplicate upgrades might tip the scales in the future but shouldn't be considered right now. If a fight is won by the player, the player receives a pull as a reward. If the challenger wins, it enters the finale draw for stage 5. The balancing should be in a way that ~50% of the fights are won by challengers. They should see the result immediately. If they win they get the amount of stars as a currency (decided later what to use for). After a fight, a character rests before it can fight again, by its rarity: 1 day for a basic one, 2 for an adventurer, 3 for a hero and 4 for a legend (one that fought on Monday with 1 rest day is back on Wednesday). Rest days carry over from one week into the next. When picking fighters, a resting character shows a moon with the days it still rests ("2d") and "Resting, back in 2 days" (or "back tomorrow"); resting ones come last in the roster, the ones back soonest first. The collection shows the same moon on every resting character's card, greyed out, every day (with a fight or not), from the moment it's picked: today's fighters rest from tomorrow on, so a legend picked today is "back in 5 days". It's sorted by **Cooldown** by default: the characters without one first, then the ones back soonest. A resting character's details say "resting, back in 2 days" (its drawing isn't greyed out there), and the **Resting** filter lists only the characters on cooldown; a character without the moon is ready. With every character resting, a player has no fight that day: **Pick your fighters** is greyed out, and tapping it says no heroes are available for fighting and when the first one is back.
# The app
The daily hub shows the 4 steps of the cycle as big buttons (icon and title, the tutorials explain each step), two by two:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1. Choose a challenger | 2. Draw a challenger |
| 3. Boost challengers | 4. Pick your fighters |

When choosing a challenger, picking a player shows only their name, no drawing of them.

The game has chiptune sound effects and music (on the web). Music and sound effects each have their own switch in the account menu (**Music: off / on**, **Sound effects: off / on**), both off until the player turns them on; the device remembers them, and the browser lets them play from the first tap. The sounds download as soon as they're turned on (or as the game opens, if they already are), so each plays right on its moment; one that still isn't ready in time is skipped rather than played late. While music is on, a short loop plays throughout, turned down on the drawing and summon screens. With sound effects on, buttons click like a mouse when pressed (the main ones, like **Ready**, **Done** or **Save**, a little fuller), the drawing clock ticks each second and beeps sharper for the last 10, a buzzer sounds when time is up and a chime once the drawing is saved; a boost star sparkles as it lands on a challenger; a fight is heard as it plays out: the challenger lands with a thud, each fighter rises with a whoosh, "Fight!" rings out, every attack swings and lands with a punch (heavier for a hard hit), and the knockout blow booms in slow motion. It ends on a fanfare when the fighters win and a sad little tune when the challenger does, Every reward chimes as its pulls and stars fly into the counter, wherever they come from: the daily steps, won fights, collected rewards and accepted gifts. An upgrade unlocking gets a sparkling fanfare. The summon has its own sounds (see [Gacha Rates](./gacha-rates.md)).

On the web, the browser's back button goes back one screen at a time (however often it's pressed in a row), like the app's own back arrow, and closes an open dialog first. Screens that can't be left right away keep their rules: a running drawing ignores it, unsaved boosts ask first. On the daily hub (or whatever screen the game starts on), back asks **Leave the game?** before leaving for the page before the game, so it never drops the player on the sign-in page by accident. On a computer, **Esc** goes back the same way, one step at a time: it closes the menu, dialog or sheet on top (the account menu, a dropdown, a tutorial…) or leaves the screen, keeping the same rules, but on the first screen it does nothing rather than ask to leave the game (it does close **Leave the game?** once back asked it). While typing in a text field, the first Esc stops typing and the next goes back.

On a computer, the screens that swipe left and right also work with the keyboard: ← and → switch banners on the summon screen, move between the challengers when boosting and turn the pages of the tutorials (doing nothing at either end), like a swipe. Boosting, ↑ gives the challenger in view a star and ↓ takes one back. Enter or Space goes on in the tutorials (**Next**, then **Got it**), and on a summon it cuts the box open, like → (see [Gacha Rates](./gacha-rates.md)). A text field being typed in keeps its keys, and a button reached with Tab presses itself on Enter. On wide screens, big ← and → buttons sit beside the summon banners (next to their dots), the challengers' star tray and the tutorial pages; phones keep to swiping.

Above them, next to the day's title and above the drawing step, a **Send a hurry** button sends the day's free hurry, with an optional short message for the target (greyed out once sent, when tapping it recalls whom you hurried and your message, or on a day without one).

Below them, an **Arena** button opens the Arena (see [The Arena](#the-arena) below), with a badge counting the votes left for the day's stars; it's greyed out in the first week ("The Arena opens next week, with this week's challengers.") and in speed mode.

Below it, a **Heroes** button opens the heroes menu with the same big buttons:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Summon** (an animated banner with the pulls ready) | |
| Hero List | Challengers |
| Rewards | Win rates |
| Fight replay (past days' fights) | Week ranking |

**Challengers** lists the challengers you drew (for now only your own, not the ones you fought or boosted), each with its record in its decided fights.

**Rewards** lists your achievements, each with your progress (e.g. "2/5"), its reward in stars and, once reached, a **Collect** button; each is collected once, and celebrated like other stars. Ready ones come first, collected ones last. Wins, streaks and close calls have levels, of which only the next one shows: the lowest not collected yet (reached or not), or the top one once every level is collected. Collecting a level brings up the next. The Rewards button, and the Heroes button on the hub, count the ones to collect among those shown.

| Achievement | Goal | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| *Sam's gallery* (one per other player) | Summon every starter hero (setup drawing) that player drew; your own are yours from the start | 15 − players ⭐: 10 with 5 players … 5 with 10 |
| *Portraits of Sam* / *Your portraits* (one per player) | Summon every starter hero portraying that player | the same |
| *Every starter Basic / Adventurer / Legend* | Summon every starter hero of that rarity | 10 / 15 / 20 ⭐ |
| *First win*, *3 wins* … *50 wins* (1, 3, 5, 10, 25, 50) | Win fights you picked the fighters for (not chance's) | 5 ⭐ each level |
| *3 wins in a row*, *4 wins in a row* … *25 wins in a row* (every number from 3 to 25) | Win that many of your fights in a row; a lost one starts over, days without one of your fights don't | 5 ⭐ each level |
| *Close Call*, *3 Close Calls* … *50 Close Calls* (1, 3, 5, 10, 25, 50) | Win that many of your fights by exactly one star, extra stars included (like 9 against 8) | 5 ⭐ each level |

Speed mode has at most three fights of your own, so it hides the levels above 3.

**Fight replay** lists the days before today that had fights of yours, with the day number and the calendar date (the first fights are on day 4, so replays start on day 5). For now a player only sees their own fights: picking a day shows the fight they picked the fighters for (not one chance picked for them) and the fight against the challenger they drew, each by its challenger and highlighted. Tapping a challenger replays its fight and shows the result. Today's own fight replays from the fight screen. The list of days is kept on the device for the day and a past day's fights for good, as they're over, so they open without asking the server again; the refresh button in the top right asks for the days anyway.

The **Hero List** shows every character of the pool, summoned or not, and can be narrowed down by rarity, by status (upgrade ready, summoned, not summoned) and by player: the drawings *of* a player or *by* a player. In the **Hero List**, a character's page has a **download** button (in the browser) that saves its drawing as a PNG image on its cardboard card, named after the character. A **Progress** button in the **Hero List** sums up how far a collection has come: a ring with the characters collected out of the whole pool, then how many owned characters are maxed out, the upgrades unlocked out of all of them, the duplicates still needed to max everything and the spare ones; below, the same by rarity (collected, upgrades and maxed out as bars). A dropdown at the top shows yours by default and lists every player of the server: picking one shows their progress, the same numbers, loaded from the server (only their totals, not which characters they own).

In the top right of the hub and the heroes menu, a **notification board** button has a badge counting what's new. For now that's **gifts**: pulls, stars or both an admin gives players outside the regular rewards, each with a message, or just a message when it gives neither. A gift's pulls and stars only count once it's accepted on the board ("Accept", or "Accept all"); a message is marked as read with "OK". Gifts don't expire. The board is loaded with the app and the day, without live updates; later it may carry other news too, like the weekly hero or patch notes.

Next to it, the **pulls** chip shows the pulls you have. New pulls, from a gift, a drawing or a win, are celebrated: the screen darkens, a big glowing ticket with "+N pulls" pops up in the middle, then flies into the chip, which counts up one pull at a time as it lands. Pulls earned on another screen (like the pull a drawing or a win earns) are celebrated once you're back on a screen with the chip and the other screen is gone; they're yours as soon as they're earned, even if you close the app before.

New stars are celebrated the same way, with a big star and "+N stars" flying into the pulls chip. Pulls and stars earned together (a drawing, a win) are celebrated together: the ticket and the star side by side, with e.g. "+1 pull · +3 stars". Spending stars on pulls isn't celebrated.

A tap on the pulls chip opens a small menu under it with your **stars** ⭐, the currency players collect. What earns them is explained in the welcome intro (and "How to play"):
- writing a challenger title (step 1): 3 stars;
- writing it for one of the players picked least this week: 1 star more. Every prompt of the week's days before today counts as a pick of the player it's for, premade titles standing in included; the players are taken fewest picks first, a whole tie at a time, as long as that makes three of them at most, so it's up to three players, and none when a tie would make more. The picker shows them with a **+1 ★** badge on their chip (never on yourself, so you may have fewer to choose from). None on the first day of a week, or in speed mode;
- drawing a challenger (step 2): 3 stars, besides its pull;
- winning a fight you picked the fighters for (step 4): the challenger's stars (its own and its boost stars), besides the pull.
- drawing a challenger that wins a fight: its stars in that fight (its own and its boost stars), whoever picked the fighters, even chance. Its artist collects them in the **Challengers** menu, on the challenger's card, from the day after the fight: the 5th day of the rotation. The Challengers button, and the Heroes button on the hub, show a badge counting the challengers with stars to collect.

**10 stars buy a pull**, any time and without limit, right when summoning: Pull ×1 and Pull ×10 work as long as the player's pulls plus the pulls their stars buy are enough, and a pull they're short of pulls for buys the missing ones with stars in the same step (e.g. a ×10 with 3 pulls spends the 3 and 70 stars). Every pull asks first, in a short confirmation dialog, **Pull heroes**: "Do you want to use", then what it spends, one line each with its icon (e.g. "8" with a ticket and "20" with a star; a line without anything to spend is left out), and "to pull 10 heroes?" (or "1 hero"), with Cancel and Pull. Pulls bought with stars count like any other for the beginner and legend guarantees. Each reward counts once. The victory stars of the fights won before stars came in carried over.

Players with admin permissions get an **Admin** menu in the app bar instead of several buttons: the game content (for content editors), and for players who manage servers, trying the mockup and **sending a gift**. The gift form sends pulls with a message to all servers (and all players), or to one server picked from a list, to all of its players or to players picked from its roster. The **Drawings** screen shows any drawing of any server, picked from three lists: the server, the date (or "Setup" for the initial drawings) and the drawing. It's shown as drawn, without upgrade effects, with a download button that saves it as a PNG.

A button that isn't available (yet) is greyed out instead of hidden, e.g. everything but step 1 on day 1. Tapping a greyed out button shows why, e.g. "Boosting starts on day 3." or "Speed mode has no win rates.".

# At the very end of the week:
At the end of the week, its top **3** challengers become **weekly heroes**, available to be pulled from the gacha pool, whatever their stars: the 1st place as a ★★★★ legend, the 2nd as a ★★★ hero and the 3rd as a ★★ adventurer. A week with fewer challengers crowns fewer, and one without any crowns none. A crowned challenger takes its new rarity for good: in pulls, upgrades, rest days and fights. Finales before this change crowned one hero, a 3☆, and keep it.

The ranking is built one place at a time, each place going to the best challenger left by:
1. its boost stars;
2. whether the player who drew it has no drawing ranked above it yet: those who don't come first, so challengers with as many boost stars spread their places across players;
3. the boost stars all the challengers of the player who drew it got that week (the same as their Grand Artist stat);
4. its win rate (fights won out of fights, none counting as 0);
5. the day it was drawn, the later the better.

A challenger with more boost stars always ranks higher, even if its artist already has a drawing above it. Challengers tied on all five (which takes two artists drawing on the same day) go by who drew theirs last.

The full rank list of challengers in order (including the amount of stars) should also be visible to the players after the week.

A challenger counts for the week of its fight, once it went through all four steps. Challengers still waiting for their fight on Sunday aren't ranked yet:

| Title | Drawing | Boosting | Fight | Ranked in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | that week |
| Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | Friday | that week |
| Wednesday | Thursday | Friday | Saturday | that week |
| Thursday | Friday | Saturday | Sunday | that week |
| Friday | Saturday | Sunday | Monday | the next week |
| Saturday | Sunday | Monday | Tuesday | the next week |
| Sunday | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | the next week |

**Week ranking** (in the heroes menu, from day 1 on; speed mode has its own final ranking instead) has three views, picked at the top: **Players**, **Challengers** and **Arena**, each for **This week** (up to today) or **Last week** (the latest finished one, Monday to Sunday). It opens on the players of this week. Weeks are named by number ("Week 1" is the launch week, Monday to Sunday, so it may be short; "Week 2" starts on the Monday after) instead of their days. Before the first week is over, last week says there's no ranking yet. This week's answers are kept on the device for the day, last week's until the next week is over, so the screen opens without asking the server again; the refresh button in the top right asks anyway.

**Players** rank every player who joined in five categories, one at a time, picked with a chip each, wrapping onto a second line where the screen is too narrow (Giant Slayer, Grand Artist, Addict, Hurry Crew, Prompt Engineer):

| Category | What counts |
|---|---|
| Giant Slayer | fights won, of the ones the player picked the fighters for (chance's don't count) |
| Grand Artist | the boost stars the challengers they drew got (chance's included), in the week of the challenger's fight, like the challengers' ranking. **Secret** this week ("Secret until the week is over"), as it would tell how strong a drawing is: only the players and their trophies show. Last week's is shown. |
| Addict | the run of days up to the week's last one, across weeks, on which they did every daily step they could themselves: their title, drawing, boosts and fight. A step with nothing to do (no prompt to draw, no challengers to boost, no challenger to fight or every hero resting) doesn't break it. This week's counts today once it's done, and today doesn't break it before. |
| Hurry Crew | the hurries the player received, each on the day it hits (the day after it was sent), whether or not they drew that day |
| Prompt Engineer | the boost stars (chance's included) the challengers drawn from titles the player wrote got, in the week of the challenger's fight, like Grand Artist; a challenger drawn from a premade title (when its author wrote none) counts for nobody. **Secret** this week, like Grand Artist. |

Every player is listed best first, with their value, without place numbers: the first places (ties share it) show a trophy at the start of their row, filled once the week is over and they won it, an outline in this week's view as it's still to win. Your own row is highlighted. When a new week begins, the first place of each category wins a **trophy**, worth **3 stars** as a gift on the notification board ("Trophy: Addict in week 1!"); tied first places each win one, and a first place of 0 wins nothing. Trophies are kept: every player shows the trophies they won in a category so far ("×2").

**Challengers** of this week start with a **Boost balance** card: the boost stars to give a day this week's challengers are boosted with, and how last week's fights changed them (see [Boost Balance](./boost-balance.md)). Then only who they portray, without any ranking or stars, which stay secret until the week is over: a pie of the challengers drawn so far for this week (the ones fought this week, see above) portraying each player, every player keeping a color of their own (by roster position, ten colors for a full roster), and a legend of every player, most chosen first, with their count and share. Last week's start with the same pie for that week, then its weekly heroes on big cards of their own, by place ("#1 · Weekly hero · ★★★★": its drawing large, its nameplate and stars, framed in the colour of the rarity it was crowned with), then the others in ranking order (see above): each with its place, a small drawing, its nameplate (the player it portrays and its title) and its stars at the end of the week. Tapping one opens its details: the drawing big (tap it for full screen, to zoom in), "Weekly hero (Legend) with 11 ★" for a weekly hero, who drew it, who wrote its prompt ("(default)" when they wrote none and it was drawn from a premade title), its boost stars in total and by player ("(default)" for the stars chance spread for a player who didn't give them), the day it was drawn with that day's theme ("Day 3 · Frozen Peaks"), the boost stars all the challengers of its artist got that week ("+7 ★ boosted"), its fight: "Won against Sam's team of heroes" (or "Lost …"), Sam being the player who picked the fighters, and its win rate ("100% · won 1 of 1"): everything its place was decided by.

**Revealing secrets**: server admins (permission `manage:servers`) see a **Reveal secrets (admin)** chip under **This week**, mainly to debug, even on servers they play on. Turned on, this week shows what everyone else only sees once it's over, in the same views as last week, without any crown yet: in **Players**, Grand Artist and Prompt Engineer with their values; in **Challengers**, this week's challengers ranked so far, with their boosts and fights up to now (nothing spread or picked by chance yet), each with its place, stars and details; in **Arena**, the open Arena ranked by its votes so far. In speed mode, **Challengers** ranks the session's challengers so far. Revealed answers are always asked anew, never kept on the device; turning the chip off shows this week as usual.

Speed mode has no player stats.

| Hurry Crew | the hurries the player received, in the week of the day each hits (the day after it was sent) |
| Prompt Engineer | the boost stars (chance's included) the challengers drawn from titles the player wrote got (not premade titles), in the week of the challenger's fight. Secret during the week, like Grand Artist. |

Tied players share a place. When a new week begins, the first place of each category wins a **trophy**, worth **3 stars** as a gift on the notification board ("Trophy: Addict in week 1!"); tied first places each win one, and a first place of 0 wins nothing. Trophies are kept, so players can see how many each won. Weeks are numbered from the launch week ("Week 1", Monday to Sunday, so it may be short). Speed mode has no trophies.

## The Arena

The Arena lets every player, not only the three who boost a challenger, decide which drawings become heroes. It lives alongside the weekly finale, which stays as it is.

| Week | What happens to week X's challengers |
|---|---|
| X | drawn, boosted and fought (they count for the week of their fight, like in the finale) |
| X+1 | the finale crowns its top 3 as weekly heroes, for the weekly banner; **the Arena opens with all the others** |
| X+2 | the Arena's top 3 become **Arena winners**, for the Arena banner; the rest leave the Arena |

So the Arena only ever holds a past week's challengers, never the present week's: the first one opens in a server's second week, with week 1's challengers, and the first Arena winners come on the Monday of the third week. The challengers that win neither are kept (not deleted), for later modes. Speed mode has no Arena.

- **Matchups**: the Arena screen shows two of its challengers on one tall card, as large as the screen allows, like a versus screen: a soft seam curves from top left to bottom right, and each half shows the scenery of its challenger's day (the theme it was drawn for). One challenger stands on its standee base top right with its nameplate beside it on the left (who it portrays, then its title, as in a fight, without stars), the other stands bottom left with its nameplate on the right. Both standees, drawing and base together, sway gently from side to side, out of step (still when the device asks for reduced motion). A round arrow sits on the outer edge of each drawing, pointing the way that picks it (→ by the one top right, ← by the one bottom left), nudging outward now and then (still as well with reduced motion). Behind the card, the screen is plain, without the day's scenery, so it doesn't get too busy. Their artists, stars and places in the finale's ranking don't show: players judge the drawing only. They drag the card toward the better one (right for the one on the right, left for the one on the left): the card tilts, the side it leans toward lights up with a "BETTER" stamp and its arrow grows, the other darkens and its arrow fades, and let go about a third of the card's width to the side (or flung at least half that far), it flies off that way; a shorter drag springs back. The whole card is what's dragged, from anywhere on it (drawings, nameplates or scenery); the hints below it say so ("← Drag the card", "Drag the card →"). On a computer, → and ← throw it the same way. **Can't decide** (or ↓), a solid button below the card, is a draw: the card drops. The next matchup already waits below, tilted a little so it peeks out, and comes up as the card goes: the server sends it along with the current one (and voting on it works even if the server would now pick another). A player never gets a matchup with a drawing of their own, and votes on each pair once. Matchups are picked by the fewest votes the two challengers have between them first, then the pair of players they portray that met the least (so every pair of players gets matchups), then the closest ratings. The vote animates the pick, then the next matchup comes in.
- **Stars and the daily limit**: a day's first **10** votes earn **3 stars**, shown with a bar on top of the screen ("4 of 10 for 3 ★", then "3 ★ earned · 12 more today"). Players can keep voting after them, unrewarded, as much as they like, as long as 10 pairs (a pack's) stay for every day of the Arena after today, so there are matchups for the daily stars until Sunday; always at least the 10, while pairs are left. Once only that reserve is left, the screen says "That's all for today". (In a 10-player server with everyone drawing, that's over 1,700 votes on the first day.)
- **Ranking**: once the week of votes is over (on Monday), the Arena's challengers are ranked by their strength from all of its votes at once (a Bradley–Terry rating: beating a strong drawing counts for more than beating a weak one, and the order of the votes doesn't matter; a draw counts a half for each, and every challenger also draws once with a virtual one, so one without votes sits in the middle), then their place in their week's ranking. Only challengers that took part in at least **5** matchups (votes by anyone, draws included) are ranked; the others come after them, unranked. Their votes still count for the ratings of the ones they met. The ratings stay secret until then. So that a better record ranked lower makes sense, each challenger also has its **opponents' strength**: the average win share of the challengers it met (their score per matchup, from all of theirs), counted once per matchup, so one met twice counts twice.
- **Arena winners**: the top 3 of the ranked challengers join the gacha pool with the finale's rarities by place: 1st a ★★★★ legend, 2nd a ★★★ hero, 3rd a ★★ adventurer. As the weekly heroes never enter the Arena, a week adds up to 6 different heroes to the pool. With fewer ranked challengers (fewer than 3 took part in 5 matchups), it crowns as many as there are; with none (not enough votes, or no challengers), it crowns nobody, and the Arena banner stays closed until an Arena crowns winners again.
- **Arena banner**: see [Gacha Rates](./gacha-rates.md).
- **Week ranking**: the **Arena** view shows, for **This week**, which week's challengers are in the Arena and how many, their ratings secret; for **Last week**, the latest decided Arena: its winners on big cards ("#1 · Arena winner · ★★★★", with who drew it, its rating as the main number, "Rating 3.27", and below it "Won 6½ of 8 matchups · opponents won 58%"), then the others with their places and ratings ("1.42", what their places follow, with two decimals), and below each its record and how often its opponents won ("3 of 7 · vs. 58%"), and last, under **Not enough matchups**, the unranked ones with their matchups ("3 of 5 matchups"). An Arena that crowned nobody says "Not enough votes: nobody was crowned this week." The intro explains that places follow the rating, where beating strong drawings counts for more, and what the line below it means. It's kept on the device until the next Arena is decided. Server admins can reveal this week's Arena ranked by its votes so far (see **Revealing secrets** above).
- **Tutorial**: the Arena opens with its own intro the first time: last week's challengers, stars for voting, and Arena winners.
