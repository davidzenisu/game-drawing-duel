The standard banner shows off three heroes of the starting pool (the setup drawings), the same all week: the basic (1 star) of one player, a 2 star of the next and the legend (4 star) of the one after, in roster order. In the server's first week that's players 1, 2 and 3; each week (Monday to Sunday) the next player starts, around the roster. A player without a drawing of that rarity hands the slot to the next one who has one. It's only what the banner shows: the pull rates don't change.

Admins can also gift pulls, stars or both outside the regular rewards, with a message (or send just a message, with neither): to everyone on every server, everyone on one server, or chosen players of one (up to 100 pulls and 1000 stars each). Players accept them on the notification board, and the pulls and stars only count once accepted (see [Premise](./premise.md)).

The gacha chances should be as follows:
- 1 star (Basics/Starters)
- 2 star (adventurers)
- 3 star (heroes)
- 4 star (legends)

The rates, with more 1 stars than 2 stars:
- 1 star: 50%
- 2 star: 40%
- 3 star: 8%
- 4 star: 2%

(At first stolen from Arknights rates: 40% 1 star and 50% 2 star, the rest the same.)

Both banners pull from the same pool: the setup drawings and every weekly hero, the up to 3 challengers each week's finale crowns, with the rarity (2 to 4 star) their stars earned them (see the [premise](./premise.md)). The weekly banner (from the first finale on) adds this week's rate-up: every hero of the latest finale and, when none of them is a 4 star, the starter legend (4 star setup drawing) portraying the 1st place's player; with several tied for 1st, one of their players' starter legends is picked at random, once per finale, so it stays the same all week. A week without heroes has no rate-up, and the weekly banner stays closed until the next finale crowns some. The standard one has the beginner guarantee. Before the first finale there's no 3 star yet: their 8% goes to the 2 stars.

Pulls come out of a cardboard **gacha box**, drawn in 3D, taped shut and stamped "Drawing Duel" (the weekly banner's is taped in gold and stamped "Weekly hero"). The camera sways gently round it while the player **cuts the tape open**: a swipe along it, left to right, the scissors following the finger (before the first swipe they slide along the tape to show what to do), fading out as the cut gets going; the last tenth cuts itself, so a swipe that falls a little short still opens the box. The phone buzzes as the cut starts and as it runs, the box trembles, and light shines out of the cut, a soft glow flaring where the blade is: white at first, brighter the further the cut, turning to the colour of the rarest pull inside only towards its end. Tapping doesn't skip the cut; the Skip button does. For a single pull the cut box then shakes, harder and longer the rarer the card, the camera pushing in, and leaks light through its seams. Then the flaps fold open with one heavy buzz of the phone, longer and heavier the rarer the best pull (from 150 ms for a ★ to 800 ms for a ★★★★; a 10-pull's box as soon as it's cut), and the light pours out in a beam, the card rises out of the box towards you as the camera tilts up after it and the box sinks away, and the card flips and bursts into its reveal. A 10-pull has one box and one cut: its cards rise out of it one after another and fan out into the carousel of glowing card backs, then each gets its reveal on its own, without a box. Once the tape is cut a tap skips ahead, as before; with animations turned off on the device, the reveal shows straight away.
